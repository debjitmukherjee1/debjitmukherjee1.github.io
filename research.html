02 · Method

How a rating gets set.

Every model carries several valuation methods and a stated blend. US names are rated against the bond yield; Indian notes are shared privately and published in full, with their outcome, once their 12-month horizon ends.

  1. ScreenCoverage starts from a specific question (a re-rating, a margin debate, a capex cycle), checked against filings before a model is opened.
  2. ModelIncome statement, balance sheet and cash flow built from the annual report, then the valuation that fits the business: DCF, excess return for banks, sum of the parts for conglomerates.
  3. ValueSeveral methods, a stated blend, sensitivities and bull and bear cases. The gap between methods is explained, not averaged away, and where one input moves a call across a band the note gives the range and the way I lean.
  4. PublishA rated note with the Excel model attached, logged on a public track record that keeps the losers.
Buy
Above the cost of equity

Expected total return clears what the stock has to earn for its risk.

Accumulate
Above the bond + 2 points

Beats the local 10-year government yield by two points, but not the full cost of equity.

Hold
Positive, below the hurdle

A return above zero that still doesn't pay enough over the bond.

Reduce
Below zero

Fair value plus the dividend sits under today's price.

Why most calls land on Hold or Reduce. The bands are set against the cost of equity, and most of the US names I cover trade at around 25 to 45 times earnings with the 10-year near 5%. At those prices a great business can still be priced to return less than its hurdle, which is usually the point the note makes. When a single input (beta, the equity risk premium, one year's EPS) would move a call across a band, the summary says so and gives both values.
